# thread0 0.1.343 (Windows)

This release adds Gemini as a third AI engine, brings Remote into the desktop app, reworks guest collaboration around one conversation per person, and makes Remote much steadier on slow mobile networks. It includes all changes since 0.1.310. The macOS stable build stays at 0.1.312 for now; its 0.1.343 build follows separately.

## Changes

### Gemini and models
- Gemini joins Claude and GPT. Sign in once with a personal Google account (the Google Antigravity CLI is prepared and verified by thread0 itself), then pick Gemini for new sessions or switch a running session to it. Every Gemini tool call goes through thread0's own approval check, the same as the other engines.
- The Gemini model picker shows each base model once with an effort level (Low, Medium, High). Work and school Google accounts that cannot use Gemini get a clear card that explains how to sign in with a personal account. Signing out of Gemini stays signed out.
- Claude Haiku 5.5 appears for accounts that have Haiku. GPT pickers show the newest version of each model family (GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra); older versions are hidden, and a session already running on one still shows its model.
- Model lists follow the same rules even when they come from a saved list, and thread0 uses the newer of its own GPT runtime and the one on this PC.

### Remote in the desktop app
- **Remote** opens inside the main window: open your other PCs or join a host as a guest without a separate window, using the same thread0 account as the desktop. Signing in there signs the desktop in too.
- When the first connection is not available, Remote says exactly why (the PC is off, it is this PC, the invitation ended) and offers only the alternatives that work. It never connects to the PC you are on, including another thread0 app on the same computer.
- Guest invitations you receive show as a badge on Remote and one line on Home.

### Guests (collaboration)
- People who are invited are now called guests, and the person who invited them the host, in all nine languages.
- One guest is one row and one conversation, even across several browsers or invitations. On a phone the conversation fills the screen.
- Guest conversations support attachments, pasted images and dictation on both sides.
- The roles are clearer: Collaborate can send and receive files unless the host turns that off; Full adds the terminal. Actions that affect the whole host PC (routines, MCP connectors, safe-mode restart) stay with the host. Hosts can raise or lower a connected guest's role, and guests get their own personal folder.

### Remote on phones and slow networks
- On slow mobile links, the PC paces what it sends to what the phone can take, so pings and replies no longer wait behind large updates. Requests that are still arriving get more time instead of failing.
- The connection notice appears only when a drop lasts, shows one line with Choose another PC and Refresh, and does not offer Reconnect while thread0 is already reconnecting. Refresh keeps the open conversation and your draft.
- Live answers stream smoothly instead of in bursts, and saving a large file on the host no longer freezes the connection for everyone.

### Organizations
- Every organization limit is visible before it blocks: engines, models, work folders, MCP connectors, the remote terminal, minimum version, update source and usage limits are locked or explained where you choose them.
- A PC leaves its organization when the account that enrolled it signs out. Engine allowlists saved before Gemini existed also allow Gemini.
- Organizations can assign media tools (built-in AI account tools or a company server) without API keys.

### Everything else
- All on-screen text comes from the nine-language dictionaries, including chat notices and service errors, which now show as readable cards with the original message folded away.
- Recent Activity keeps results you have not opened yet, even after the session closes or the app restarts.
- One send button and consistent tooltips across the desktop and Remote.
- New installs keep their work in a `thread0` folder under Documents; existing installs keep their current folder.
